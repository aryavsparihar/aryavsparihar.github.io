About

My path into research started with a practical problem. In the Chenab Valley of Jammu and Kashmir, I built Nida-e-Watan, an offline AI system for villages with only a couple of hours of internet a day. Fitting language models onto a small cluster of Raspberry Pis taught me that model compression and hardware-software co-design decide whether AI is usable at all outside a data centre.

That question led to my current work as a Research and Development Intern at the University of Ljubljana, Slovenia, with Prof. Veljko Pejović. I measure what language model inference really costs in energy and carbon, and I am building a scheduler that decides where each query should run: on the device, at the edge, or in the cloud.

The next step brings this work closer to people. From January 2027, as a Visiting Researcher at the Cognitive Interface Lab, Nanyang Technological University Singapore, I will work on wearable systems that read biosignals and respond in real time, where every milliwatt and every kilobyte of memory counts.

Across all of this, I care about systems that stay robust, secure, and interpretable once they leave the lab, so that people can trust the AI they carry with them.

I am pursuing a Bachelor of Technology in Computer Science and Engineering at Shri Mata Vaishno Devi University in Jammu and Kashmir, India.

Sustainable computing Ongoing

Carbon-Aware Scheduling for Large Language Model Inference

University of Ljubljana, with Prof. Veljko Pejović · 2026 – present

Hardware tiers
DeviceApple M4 laptopmeasured
EdgeRTX 3070 desktopnext
Data centreNVIDIA A100next

Interactive queries can't be postponed until the grid is cleaner. So instead of shifting when a query runs, this scheduler shifts where it runs (on the user's device, an edge server, or a data centre) and how (model size and quantisation). It reduces emissions while keeping time-to-first-token, time per output token, and answer quality within set limits. The work rests on a measurement harness that profiles Qwen2.5 models served with llama.cpp on each hardware tier.

Early findings from the device tier (Apple M4 laptop)

  • ≈10×Generating a token costs about ten times more energy than reading one.
  • −53%Batching 16 requests cuts energy per query, but waiting time to the first token rises twelvefold.
  • ≈4×From Slovenia, choosing where a query runs within a 30 ms latency budget saves about four times more carbon than delaying it by 24 hours.
  • 0.3%Energy per query reproduces across three days to within 0.3% variation.

Next: edge (RTX 3070) and data-centre (A100) measurements, the cost model, and the scheduler itself.

Python · llama.cpp · powermetrics · ENTSO-E carbon data · statistics

Security

Behaviour-Based Detection of USB Charging Threats

Shri Mata Vaishno Devi University · 2026

A detection pipeline and telemetry simulator for juice jacking, keystroke injection, class-swapping devices, and concealed hardware, built on 60,000 simulated sessions. On chargers and devices it never saw, the detector reaches 80% recall at a 1% false-positive budget, with high variance across seeds. It scores 98% on familiar hardware, against 77% for a rule baseline. The scorer exports to 27 KB of dependency-free C. All results are on synthetic data, and the project audits and retracts an earlier version's inflated numbers.

Gradient boosting · SHAP · ONNX · C export

Edge AI

Nida-e-Watan: Offline Edge Inference Cluster

2025 – present

An offline-first AI system across 12 Raspberry Pi 4 nodes, serving villages with about two hours of connectivity a day. Quantising and pruning multilingual BERT and IndicBERT shrank the model from 1.1 GB to 260 MB while keeping 91% task accuracy. On-device classification takes 380 ms, and conflict-free replicated data types keep 99.2% data integrity between uplinks.

Raspberry Pi · ONNX Runtime · quantisation · pruning

2026

A cloud-free biosensing patch built on an Arduino Nano with electrochemical electrodes and temperature sensors. A time-series classifier runs on the microcontroller itself, within its memory limits, to flag physiological anomalies in real time.

Perception

Multispectral Terrain Classification & Multi-Agent Routing

2026

A ResNet-18 using five spectral bands beat one using only colour (97.76% vs 97.06%), checked across random seeds and explained with Grad-CAM. The classified terrain then fed an A* router, where convoy, recon, and stealth agents re-plan around soil moisture, visibility, and a simulated rainfall event.
