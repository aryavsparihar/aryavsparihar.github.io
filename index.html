# about

I'm a Computer Science undergraduate at SMVDU working on getting foundation models to run on the physical edge. That means fitting them onto the microcontrollers and single-board computers inside autonomous and embodied systems, where there is no cloud to fall back on.

I work on hardware-software co-design: compressing, adapting, and localizing models (quantization, pruning, distillation) so they run in real time on microcontroller-class and single-board hardware. My work spans edge-native sensor fusion and tightly coupled perception-action pipelines for robotics, human-robot interaction, and agents operating in dynamic, GPS-denied environments.

Increasingly, I care about the robustness, security, and interpretability of these systems. I want autonomous agents deployed under real-world constraints to stay trustworthy, auditable, and resilient to adversarial conditions. Outside the lab, I founded Project Nida-e-Watan, a digital-inclusion initiative in the Chenab Valley.

# projects

Nida-e-Watan: Distributed Edge Inference

Jan 2025 – present

An offline-first edge inference system running across a 12-node Raspberry Pi 4 cluster with no reliable uplink. I built the whole stack, from hardware selection and OS configuration to model serving and inter-node communication.

  • 8-bit quantization and structured pruning on BERT-multilingual and IndicBERT: footprint cut from 1.1 GB to 260 MB (4.2×) while keeping 91% task accuracy.
  • ONNX Runtime serving: 380 ms classification and 1.2 s generative inference on a 4 GB Pi under continuous load.
  • CRDT-based sync for eventual consistency over a 2-hour daily uplink window, with 99.2% data integrity across the cluster.

Eco-Smart Wearable Biosensing Patch

Feb 2026 – May 2026

A full hardware-software co-design cycle for an embedded biosensing system, with sensing, compute, and output on a single low-power device and no cloud offload.

  • Acquisition layer: Arduino Nano with electrochemical copper electrodes and continuous temperature sensors for real-time physiological signal capture.
  • A lightweight time-series classifier deployed on the microcontroller itself, fusing voltage and thermal streams within the Nano's SRAM and flash limits.
  • A closed-loop, acquisition-to-inference design that can serve as a template for embodied AI systems where latency and privacy rule out the cloud.
